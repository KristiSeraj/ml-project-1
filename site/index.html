A passenger has just got into the car. This model estimates the arrival time from what is knowable at that moment — the clock, the two taxi zones, and the length of the planned route — and then turns it into a time the trip beats on nine journeys out of ten.

The honest part

The model was trained on trip_distance, which the meter measures during the trip — so it already contains whatever detour actually happened. No app has that number at pickup. This page therefore estimates the route from the zone shapefile: centre to centre in a straight line, times a detour factor of — learned on May. That estimate is itself wrong by a median of — miles, and that error is on top of every number below.

The project's scores were measured with the meter's own distance. They are therefore better than this page can truly deliver, and that is stated here for the same reason it is stated in the problem card: it is the biggest caveat on the work.

    How it works

    The target
    Drop-off time minus pickup time, in minutes. Both come from the meter, and neither is a feature — the drop-off time is the answer.
    The split
    By pickup month, not a shuffle: . The model will always run on a month it has not seen.
    Nothing from the end of the trip
    The meter charges for time, so the fare contains the answer. The fare, the tip, the total, every fee, the payment type and the rate code are refused by an allow-list and a guard that raises.
    The model
    A single decision tree, depth , nodes, fitted on May trips. Depth was chosen on June.
    In the browser
    No backend and no ML runtime: the tree and the preprocessor's fitted numbers are exported to model.json (), and model.js walks it. node site/parity.js checks those predictions against scikit-learn on 2,000 June trips.
